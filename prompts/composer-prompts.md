Fully written, ready-to-run prompts for in-house and corporate counsel.
Paste one in, attach or pin the relevant document, and fill in any `[BRACKETED]` facts.

## Instant analysis

### Should I sign this? (instant triage)

> Should I sign this contract? Triage it for me as the [customer / vendor]: give a clear sign / negotiate / escalate recommendation up front, then the handful of terms that genuinely create risk for us, ranked most to least severe, the standard protections this draft is missing, and the deal-breaker points I should push back on before signing. Ground every flag in the contract text.

### What changed between two versions?

> What changed between these two versions? Compare the earlier draft against the counterparty's latest turn and surface only the material changes - added, removed, and amended terms that move rights, obligations, liability, money, or deadlines - and tell me which side each change favors. Call out any small edit that quietly shifts risk onto us. (Pin both documents.)

### Is this compliant with GDPR / CCPA?

> Is the @-mentioned document compliant with [GDPR / CCPA / HIPAA / DPDP]? Assess it against the regulation, give an overall compliance status, and list each gap with the specific provision it implicates, the risk if left unaddressed, and a concrete fix - ordered from highest to lowest risk.

### My obligations and deadlines

> What are all my obligations and deadlines in the @-mentioned contract? Build a complete table of every obligation that falls on us - the obligation, who owes it, the triggering event, the due date or notice period, and the source clause - and call out the renewal, termination-notice, payment, and reporting deadlines specifically so none of them slip.

### Which contracts auto-renew?

> Which of these contracts auto-renew, and what is the notice period for each? Check every contract in scope and give me a per-document answer - the renewal mechanics and the exact non-renewal notice window - so I can calendar the deadlines, then flag any that renew within the next 90 days. (Pin the contracts or open a matter.)

### Review against our playbook

> Review the @-mentioned contract against our playbook and show every deviation from our standard positions. For each clause that departs from where we normally land, quote the contract's current language, state our standard position, explain the gap and why it matters, and separately list the protections our playbook expects that are missing here.

## Contract review

### Find missing protections

> Review the @-mentioned agreement and tell me what a careful in-house lawyer would expect in this type of contract that is missing or drafted too thinly here. Cover at least governing law, termination, limitation of liability, confidentiality, indemnity, and dispute resolution. For each gap, explain why it matters to us as the [our role].

### Audit indemnity and liability

> From the @-mentioned contract, extract the full indemnification and limitation-of-liability scheme. Quote each relevant clause, then tell me: who indemnifies whom and for what, the liability cap and what it is tied to, every carve-out that escapes the cap, and whether any exposure is effectively uncapped. Flag anything that puts us at one-sided or unlimited risk.

### Check IP and data clauses

> We are the [customer / vendor]. From the @-mentioned agreement, pull every clause touching IP ownership, license grants, work product, feedback, and use of our data. Quote each clause with its section number and tell me whether our pre-existing IP stays ours and whether the other side gets any rights to our data beyond what is needed to deliver the service.

### Sweep an NDA for hidden riders

> Read the @-mentioned NDA and find anything beyond ordinary confidentiality that has been buried in it. Specifically quote any residuals clause, non-solicit, non-compete, IP assignment, exclusivity, or perpetual obligation, and tell me whether the confidentiality period is market-standard for this context. If none of these are present, say so explicitly.

## Drafting

### Draft from a deal brief

> Draft a full [agreement type] in which we are the [our role], governed by [jurisdiction] law, with these in-house positions baked in: liability capped at [amount], mutual confidentiality, our IP stays ours, and [other key terms]. Use clear section headers and numbered clauses, leave [BRACKETED] placeholders for any term I did not give you, do not invent commercial terms, and end with a short list of open items I still need to decide.

### Draft a limitation of liability clause

> Draft a limitation-of-liability clause for a [agreement type] where we are the [our role], governed by [jurisdiction] law. Cap aggregate liability at [amount or formula], exclude indirect and consequential damages for both sides, and uncap liability only for [confidentiality breach, IP infringement, indemnity obligations]. Keep the language mutual unless I say otherwise.

### Draft an indemnity clause

> Draft an indemnification clause for a [agreement type] in which [counterparty] indemnifies us against third-party claims that our use of their product infringes a third party's [IP type], governed by [jurisdiction] law. Include defense obligations, notice and cooperation requirements, control of the defense, and standard exclusions. Mark any commercial figures as [BRACKETED] placeholders.

### Draft a data processing addendum

> Draft a data processing addendum for a [vendor type] that processes [type of personal data] on our behalf under [GDPR / DPDP / CCPA]. Include subject matter and duration, processor obligations, subprocessor approval and flow-down, security measures, breach-notification timeline, audit rights, and deletion or return on termination. Leave party details and timelines as [BRACKETED] placeholders.

### Convert one-way NDA to mutual

> Convert the @-mentioned one-way NDA into a balanced mutual NDA where both parties carry the same confidentiality obligations and rights. Then list, as a short summary, every change you made and any term that is still off-market or unfavorable to us as the [receiving / disclosing] party.

## Negotiation and redlining

### Build a clause playbook

> Analyze the @-mentioned [agreement type] and build a reusable negotiation playbook for us as the [customer / vendor]. For each key clause (liability, indemnity, IP, termination, warranty, confidentiality, governing law), give three tiers: Preferred (our ideal language), Acceptable (a fair compromise), and Deal-breaker (the minimum we must insist on). Present it as a table.

### Prep a negotiation ask list

> Based on the @-mentioned contract and the fact that we are the [our role], produce a negotiation brief with our top five asks ranked by importance. Format as a table with columns: ask, why it matters to us, opening position, target, walk-away, and a trade we could offer. Treat walk-away points as suggestions for attorney review, not final positions.

### Predict counterparty objections

> We want to push back on [clause or term] in the @-mentioned agreement. Predict the three objections the counterparty is most likely to raise, in priority order. For each, give the objection in their words, why they are raising it, our recommended response, and a fallback if they hold firm.

## Research and memo

### Write a research memo

> Write a short legal memo on the following question for a company operating in [jurisdiction]: [question]. Structure it as issue, short answer, analysis, and practical recommendation. State the source for every legal proposition and clearly separate what is well-settled from what is uncertain. If you are not confident a citation is real, flag it rather than stating it.

### Explain a regulation to the business

> Explain [statute or regulation] in plain English for a non-lawyer business audience: what it requires, who it applies to, the main obligations, and the consequences of getting it wrong. Cite the specific provisions you rely on, and end with three concrete actions our company should take to stay compliant.

## Compliance and regulatory

### Audit a privacy policy

> Review the @-mentioned privacy policy against [GDPR / DPDP / CCPA] and flag every required disclosure that is missing, inadequate, or out of date. For each finding, name the requirement, quote the relevant part of the policy (or note its absence), cite the provision that mandates it, and suggest fix language.

### Review an employment agreement

> Review the @-mentioned employment agreement for an employee based in [state or country]. Flag every term that is unenforceable, off-market, or risky under local law, with the restrictive covenants, IP assignment, notice periods, and termination provisions called out specifically. Quote each flagged clause and explain the risk to us as the employer.

## Corporate and governance

### Draft board resolutions

> Draft board resolutions for [the action, e.g., approving a financing round / appointing an officer / authorizing a contract] for a [entity type] incorporated in [jurisdiction]. Include the recitals, the resolved clauses, and any officer authorizations needed to carry it out. Leave names, dates, and amounts as [BRACKETED] placeholders, and note any approval that may require a shareholder vote.

### Summarize for the board

> Summarize the @-mentioned [contract or matter] for a board or executive audience in under one page. Cover what it is, the key commercial terms, the main legal risks, the decision being asked of them, and a clear recommendation. Write in plain business language and avoid legal jargon, but quote any term that is genuinely material to the decision.

## Summarize and extract

### Extract business terms

> Read the @-mentioned agreement and extract the commercial terms a non-lawyer business owner needs in a clean table: parties, term length and renewal, fees and payment timing, liability cap, termination rights, exclusivity or non-compete, and any auto-renewal or price-increase mechanics. Where a term is not stated, write "not specified" rather than guessing.

## Disputes

### Draft a demand or breach letter

> Draft a firm but professional letter to [counterparty] regarding their breach of the @-mentioned agreement. The facts are: [describe]. Include the parties, a clear statement of the breach with the specific clause cited and quoted, the cure or remedy we demand, a response deadline, and a reservation of rights. Do not assert facts I have not given you.
