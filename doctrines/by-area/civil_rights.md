# Civil Rights

5 doctrine(s) in the Civil Rights area.

General reference, not legal advice.

## EEOC filing deadline (300/180 days) - National R.R. v. Morgan continuing violations

Discrete discriminatory or retaliatory acts under Title VII must be filed with the EEOC within 300 days (in deferral states) or 180 days; hostile-work-environment claims accrue under the continuing-violations doctrine.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan | 536 U.S. 101 | 2002 | Supreme Court of the United States | Discrete acts (termination, failure to promote, denial of transfer) are independent unlawful employment practices each of which must be timely charged. Hostile-work-environment claims may include conduct outside the limitations window if at least one act contributing to the claim falls within the window. |

Canonical statutes: 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-5(e)(1).

## Groff v. DeJoy - Title VII religious accommodation standard

An employer denying a religious accommodation under Title VII must show that the accommodation would impose 'substantial increased costs' in the conduct of its business, not merely a 'de minimis' burden.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Groff v. DeJoy | 600 U.S. 447 | 2023 | Supreme Court of the United States | Title VII's 'undue hardship' standard for religious accommodations requires the employer to show that the burden of the accommodation would result in substantial increased costs, clarifying (and limiting) the prior 'de minimis' reading of TWA v. Hardison. |
| Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison | 432 U.S. 63 | 1977 | Supreme Court of the United States | Original (now-narrowed) statement that an employer need not bear more than a de minimis cost to accommodate an employee's religious practice. After Groff, the 'de minimis' language is rejected and replaced with a substantial-increased-costs test. |

Canonical statutes: 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(j) (Title VII - religion definition); 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(a) (Title VII - unlawful employment practices).

## McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework (Title VII)

The three-step burden-shifting framework for Title VII employment-discrimination claims.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green | 411 U.S. 792 | 1973 | Supreme Court of the United States | Plaintiff establishes a prima facie case; the burden shifts to the defendant to articulate a legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason; the plaintiff may then show that reason is pretext for discrimination. |
| Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine | 450 U.S. 248 | 1981 | Supreme Court of the United States | The defendant's burden of articulating a nondiscriminatory reason is one of production, not persuasion; the ultimate burden of proving intentional discrimination remains with the plaintiff. |
| St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks | 509 U.S. 502 | 1993 | Supreme Court of the United States | Even when a plaintiff proves the employer's stated reason is pretextual, the factfinder is not required to find for the plaintiff; the plaintiff retains the ultimate burden of proving intentional discrimination. |

Canonical statutes: 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2.

## Muldrow v. City of St. Louis - Title VII 'some harm' standard for adverse action

A Title VII discriminatory transfer claim requires only 'some harm' to an identifiable term or condition of employment, not a 'significant' or 'material' adverse change.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Muldrow v. City of St. Louis | 601 U.S. 346 | 2024 | Supreme Court of the United States | A Title VII plaintiff challenging a job transfer must show only 'some harm' respecting an identifiable term or condition of employment - not a 'significant', 'serious', or 'material' adverse action. |
| Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. White | 548 U.S. 53 | 2006 | Supreme Court of the United States | Title VII's anti-retaliation provision is not limited to ultimate employment actions; a 'materially adverse' action that might dissuade a reasonable worker from making a charge of discrimination is sufficient. |

## Qualified immunity

The federal common-law defense protecting government officials sued for damages under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 unless they violated clearly established law.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harlow v. Fitzgerald | 457 U.S. 800 | 1982 | Supreme Court of the United States | Government officials performing discretionary functions are shielded from liability insofar as their conduct does not violate clearly established statutory or constitutional rights of which a reasonable person would have known. |
| Pearson v. Callahan | 555 U.S. 223 | 2009 | Supreme Court of the United States | Courts may exercise discretion in deciding which of the two qualified-immunity prongs (constitutional violation vs. clearly established) to address first; the Saucier sequencing is no longer mandatory. |
| Saucier v. Katz | 533 U.S. 194 | 2001 | Supreme Court of the United States | Originally required courts to decide qualified-immunity claims via mandatory two-step sequence (constitutional violation, then clearly established law); sequencing later made discretionary in Pearson. |

Canonical statutes: 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
