## Alice/Mayo two-step (35 USC 101)

The two-step test for patent subject-matter eligibility under 35 U.S.C. § 101.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International | 573 U.S. 208 | 2014 | Supreme Court of the United States | Step 1: determine whether the claims are directed to a patent-ineligible concept (abstract idea, law of nature, natural phenomenon). Step 2: if so, search for an inventive concept that transforms the claim into patent-eligible subject matter. |
| Mayo Collaborative Services v. Prometheus Laboratories, Inc. | 566 U.S. 66 | 2012 | Supreme Court of the United States | A claim that recites a law of nature and adds only well-understood, routine, conventional activity is not patent-eligible; the inventive concept must be more than the natural law itself. |
| Bilski v. Kappos | 561 U.S. 593 | 2010 | Supreme Court of the United States | The 'machine-or-transformation' test is a useful clue but not the sole test for patent eligibility; held a hedging method to be an unpatentable abstract idea. |
| Diamond v. Diehr | 450 U.S. 175 | 1981 | Supreme Court of the United States | A process for curing rubber that incorporated a mathematical formula and the Arrhenius equation was patent-eligible because the claims as a whole performed a function the patent laws were designed to protect. |

Canonical statutes: 35 U.S.C. § 101.
