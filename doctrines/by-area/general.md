## BIPA and CUBI - biometric privacy state statutes

Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act creates a private right of action and statutory damages of $1,000 / $5,000 per violation; Texas's CUBI provides only state AG enforcement. The statutes differ sharply on damages, accrual, and consent mechanics.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rosenbach v. Six Flags Entertainment Corp. | 129 N.E.3d 1197 | 2019 | Supreme Court of Illinois | A BIPA plaintiff need not allege an actual injury beyond the statutory violation of the right to control biometric data; technical violations alone confer standing to sue under § 20. |
| Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc. | 216 N.E.3d 918 | 2023 | Supreme Court of Illinois | Separate claims accrue under BIPA each time a private entity scans or transmits biometric identifiers without complying with §§ 15(b) and 15(d) - opening employers to potentially massive aggregate damages. |

Canonical statutes: 740 ILCS 14/15 (BIPA); 740 ILCS 14/20 (BIPA - right of action); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 503.001 (CUBI).

## Business judgment rule (Delaware)

The business judgment rule: a rebuttable presumption that Delaware corporate directors, in making a business decision, acted on an informed basis, in good faith, and in the honest belief the action was in the corporation's best interest; rebutted by gross negligence, bad faith, or a disabling conflict of interest.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aronson v. Lewis | 473 A.2d 805 | 1984 | Supreme Court of Delaware | The business judgment rule is a presumption that in making a business decision the directors of a corporation acted on an informed basis, in good faith, and in the honest belief that the action taken was in the best interests of the company. Absent an abuse of discretion, that judgment will be respected by the courts. |
| Smith v. Van Gorkom | 488 A.2d 858 | 1985 | Supreme Court of Delaware | To invoke the business judgment rule's protection directors must inform themselves, prior to making a business decision, of all material information reasonably available to them; the presumption is rebutted by gross negligence, including a failure to be adequately informed. |

## Caremark / Marchand - director oversight liability under Delaware law

Directors of Delaware corporations face oversight liability under In re Caremark / Marchand when they utterly fail to implement reporting systems or consciously disregard known risks - particularly 'mission critical' regulatory risks.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation | 698 A.2d 959 | 1996 | Court of Chancery of Delaware | Directors may be liable for failing to ensure that an information and reporting system exists and provides timely, accurate information for compliance and risk monitoring. Caremark liability requires showing utterly failed oversight or conscious disregard. |
| Stone v. Ritter | 911 A.2d 362 | 2006 | Supreme Court of Delaware | Caremark oversight failures sound in good faith - a director acts in bad faith only by knowing failure to discharge the duty to monitor; held that good-faith reliance on the auditor and internal compliance systems insulated the directors there. |
| Marchand v. Barnhill | 212 A.3d 805 | 2019 | Supreme Court of Delaware | Where food safety was 'mission critical' to an ice cream company, the board's failure to establish any board-level system for monitoring food safety stated a Caremark claim. |

## Consideration - forbearance of a legal right

## Eggshell-skull / thin-skull rule

The tort rule that a defendant is liable for the full extent of harm caused, even if unforeseeably aggravated by the plaintiff's pre-existing vulnerability.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vosburg v. Putney | 50 N.W. 403 | 1891 | Supreme Court of Wisconsin | The tortfeasor is liable for all injuries directly resulting from the wrongful act, including those exacerbated by the plaintiff's pre-existing condition; the defendant takes the plaintiff as he finds him. |
| Smith v. Leech Brain & Co. | [1962] 2 QB 405 | 1962 | Queen's Bench Division (England) | English authority widely cited in U.S. tort treatises: where a worker's burn from molten metal triggered a latent cancer that killed him, the defendant was liable for the full extent of harm. Type of injury, not its extent, must be foreseeable. |
| Benn v. Thomas | 512 N.W.2d 537 | 1994 | Supreme Court of Iowa | Reversed for failure to give an eggshell-plaintiff jury instruction where a coronary-disease victim died after a relatively minor rear-end collision; reaffirms that a tortfeasor takes the plaintiff as found, with all pre-existing frailties. |

Canonical statutes: Restatement (Second) of Torts § 461.

## Implied warranty of habitability (residential leases)

A residential lease carries an implied warranty of habitability, measured by the housing code, on which the tenant's duty to pay rent depends.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Javins v. ## ITC § 337 - patent enforcement and exclusion orders

Section 337 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. § 1337) authorizes the International Trade Commission to investigate and bar imports that infringe U.S. patents, copyrights, trademarks, or trade secrets; the ITC's in rem exclusion orders are independent of district-court remedies.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suprema, Inc. v. International Trade Commission | 796 F.3d 1338 | 2015 | Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit | En banc Federal Circuit held that the ITC may bar imports under § 337 based on induced infringement of method claims even if direct infringement occurs only after importation. |
| Spansion, Inc. v. International Trade Commission | 629 F.3d 1331 | 2010 | Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit | Once a § 337 violation is established, exclusion orders issue as a matter of course; the public-interest factors limiting exclusion are rarely dispositive. |

Canonical statutes: 19 U.S.C. § 1337.

## Loper Bright (overruling Chevron deference)

The overruling of Chevron deference in favor of independent judicial judgment under the APA.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo | 603 U.S. 369 | 2024 | Supreme Court of the United States | Chevron is overruled. Under the APA, courts must exercise independent judgment in determining whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority; courts may not defer to an agency interpretation merely because the statute is ambiguous. |
| Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. | 467 U.S. 837 | 1984 | Supreme Court of the United States | Historical context: established the now-overruled framework requiring courts to defer to permissible agency interpretations of ambiguous statutes. |

## Mutual mistake (contract rescission)

The doctrine permitting rescission of a contract entered into under mutual mistake of a basic fact.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sherwood v. Walker | 33 N.W. 919 | 1887 | Supreme Court of Michigan | Where both parties were mistaken about a fact going to the substance of the contract, the contract may be avoided; later refined by Restatement § 152's three-element test (basic assumption, material effect, risk allocation). |
| Wood v. Boynton | 25 N.W. 42 | 1885 | Supreme Court of Wisconsin | Sale of a stone later discovered to be an uncut diamond could not be rescinded; both parties were ignorant of its true nature, and absent fraud or mistake about the identity of the thing sold, the bargain stands. |
| Lenawee County Board of Health v. Messerly | 331 N.W.2d 203 | 1982 | Supreme Court of Michigan | Modernized Sherwood by adopting the Restatement (Second) approach: rescission depends on whether the parties' mistake concerned a basic assumption that materially affects the agreed exchange, with allocation of risk being decisive. |

Canonical statutes: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 152; Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 154.

## Online contract formation (browsewrap, clickwrap, sign-in wrap)

Enforceability of online terms turns on whether the user had reasonably conspicuous notice of the terms and manifested assent; clickwrap is routinely enforced, browsewrap rarely so.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meyer v. Uber Technologies, Inc. | 868 F.3d 66 | 2017 | Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit | Uber's sign-in-wrap was enforceable: the registration screen provided reasonably conspicuous notice that creating an account constituted assent to the linked terms, including the arbitration clause. |
| Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. | 306 F.3d 17 | 2002 | Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit | Browsewrap terms requiring a user to scroll below the download button to find them did not bind users who had no actual or constructive notice; mere availability is not assent. |
| Sgouros v. TransUnion Corp. | 817 F.3d 1029 | 2016 | Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit | Online terms were unenforceable where the website's design suggested clicking 'I Accept' related to authorizing a credit check, not assent to the linked terms of service. |

## Promissory estoppel

The doctrine permitting enforcement of a promise that induces reasonable, detrimental reliance.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hoffman v. Red Owl Stores, Inc. | 133 N.W.2d 267 | 1965 | Supreme Court of Wisconsin | Promissory estoppel applies where a promise was made expecting reliance, induced reasonable reliance, and injustice can be avoided only by enforcing the promise; reliance damages, not expectation damages, are usually awarded. |
| Drennan v. Star Paving Co. | 51 Cal. 2d 409 | 1958 | Supreme Court of California | A subcontractor's bid to a general contractor is irrevocable for a reasonable time once the general relies on it in submitting its own bid; promissory estoppel binds the subcontractor. |

## State-law execution of wills (Florida v. New York)

State will-execution formalities vary: Florida (Fla. Stat. § 732.502) requires two witnesses present at signing; New York (N.Y. EPTL § 3-2.1) similarly requires two witnesses, but with different attestation mechanics; New York also recognizes very narrow holographic-will exceptions under § 3-2.2.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In re Estate of Wiltbank | 986 So. 2d 1289 | 2008 | District Court of Appeal of Florida, Fourth District | Florida strictly enforces § 732.502 will-formality requirements; substantial compliance is insufficient if the statutory witness-presence requirement is not satisfied. |

Canonical statutes: Fla. Stat. § 732.502; N.Y. EPTL § 3-2.1; N.Y. EPTL § 3-2.2.

## SuperValu / Safeco - subjective scienter under the False Claims Act and FCRA

Scienter under the False Claims Act and Fair Credit Reporting Act looks to what the defendant subjectively believed; an objectively reasonable interpretation does not categorically defeat scienter if the defendant actually believed otherwise.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| United States ex rel. Schutte v. SuperValu Inc. | 598 U.S. 739 | 2023 | Supreme Court of the United States | Under the False Claims Act, scienter is judged by what the defendant subjectively believed; an after-the-fact objectively reasonable interpretation of an ambiguous regulation does not categorically defeat scienter if the defendant did not actually hold that belief. |
| Safeco Insurance Co. of America v. Burr | 551 U.S. 47 | 2007 | Supreme Court of the United States | FCRA 'willful' violations include reckless disregard, but a defendant's interpretation of an ambiguous statute is not reckless if objectively reasonable and not foreclosed by authoritative guidance. |

## Waiver of fiduciary duties in Delaware LLCs (6 Del. C. § 18-1101)

Delaware permits LLC operating agreements to expand, restrict, or eliminate fiduciary duties, provided the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing is preserved (§ 18-1101(c)). The implied covenant itself may not be waived.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manti Holdings, LLC v. Authentix Acquisition Co. | 261 A.3d 1199 | 2021 | Supreme Court of Delaware | Sophisticated parties to a Delaware LLC agreement may by clear contractual language waive even statutory appraisal rights; freedom of contract is a paramount principle under the DLLC Act, but waivers must be clear and unambiguous. |
| Wood v. U.S. Bank National Ass'n | 246 A.3d 141 | 2021 | Court of Chancery of Delaware | Confirmed that under § 18-1101(c), LLC agreements may eliminate fiduciary duties so long as the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing remains; Court of Chancery enforces explicit waivers consistent with statute. |
| Miller v. HCP Trumpet Investments, LLC | 194 A.3d 908 | 2018 | Supreme Court of Delaware | Implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing cannot be waived in an LLC agreement; it operates as a gap-filler when the contract is silent, and parties may not contract around it. |

Canonical statutes: 6 Del. C. § 18-1101(c).
