## Corporate attorney-client privilege (Upjohn)

The scope of the corporate attorney-client privilege; rejection of the narrow control-group test.

Landmark cases:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upjohn Co. v. United States | 449 U.S. 383 | 1981 | Supreme Court of the United States | The corporate attorney-client privilege is not limited to a 'control group' of senior management; it protects communications between counsel and employees at any level made, at the direction of superiors, to secure legal advice for the corporation. |

## FRE 702 - December 2023 amendment and Daubert/Kumho

Rule 702 as amended effective December 1, 2023 makes explicit that the proponent must establish admissibility by a preponderance of the evidence and that the expert's opinion must reflect a reliable application of methods to facts. The amendment confirms (but does not change) the gatekeeping function under Daubert / Kumho Tire.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc. | 509 U.S. 579 | 1993 | Supreme Court of the United States | Federal Rule of Evidence 702 displaced the Frye 'general acceptance' test; judges serve as gatekeepers to ensure expert testimony rests on a reliable foundation and is relevant to the task. |
| Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael | 526 U.S. 137 | 1999 | Supreme Court of the United States | Daubert's gatekeeping obligation applies to ALL expert testimony, not just scientific testimony; the trial court has broad latitude in deciding how to assess reliability of technical and other specialized expertise. |

Canonical statutes: Fed. R. Evid. 702 (amended Dec. 1, 2023).

## Hearsay rule (FRE 801-807)

The federal evidentiary rule barring out-of-court statements offered for the truth of the matter asserted, and its many exceptions and exemptions under FRE 801 through 807.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crawford v. Washington | 541 U.S. 36 | 2004 | Supreme Court of the United States | Testimonial out-of-court statements are inadmissible against a criminal defendant under the Confrontation Clause unless the declarant is unavailable and the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine; overruled Roberts's reliability-based framework. |
| Davis v. Washington | 547 U.S. 813 | 2006 | Supreme Court of the United States | Statements made to police are testimonial when their primary purpose is to establish past events for later prosecution, but nontestimonial when made to enable police to meet an ongoing emergency. |
| Williamson v. United States | 512 U.S. 594 | 1994 | Supreme Court of the United States | FRE 804(b)(3)'s statement-against-interest exception covers only the precise statements that are individually self-inculpatory, not a broader narrative containing both self-inculpatory and non-self-inculpatory parts. |
| Ohio v. Roberts | 448 U.S. 56 | 1980 | Supreme Court of the United States | Historically held that hearsay was admissible against a criminal defendant if it bore adequate indicia of reliability; later overruled by Crawford for testimonial statements but remains important historical context. |
