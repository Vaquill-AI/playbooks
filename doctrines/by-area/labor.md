## Comparative state law on non-compete enforceability

Enforceability of non-compete clauses varies dramatically: California, North Dakota, Oklahoma, and Minnesota broadly ban most non-competes; New York, Massachusetts and many states enforce reasonable non-competes; the FTC's 2024 non-compete rule is enjoined.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP | 44 Cal. 4th 937 | 2008 | Supreme Court of California | California Business & Professions Code § 16600 voids non-compete contracts categorically; California does not recognize a narrow-restraint exception. Even tailored, reasonable post-employment non-competes are unenforceable. |
| BDO Seidman v. Hirshberg | 93 N.Y.2d 382 | 1999 | New York Court of Appeals | Under New York law, a post-employment non-compete is enforceable only if (a) reasonable in time and geographic scope, (b) no greater than required to protect a legitimate employer interest, (c) not unduly harmful to the employee, and (d) not injurious to the public. |

Canonical statutes: Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600; N.Y. Lab. Law § 5-336 (proposed, vetoed Dec 2023); FTC Non-Compete Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 910) - STATUS: VACATED.

## FMLA eligibility (29 USC 2611)

Eligibility requirements for unpaid leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc. | 535 U.S. 81 | 2002 | Supreme Court of the United States | Confirms FMLA eligibility requirements (12 months / 1,250 hours / 50-employee worksite) and limits DOL regulations that disregard the statute's text. |
| Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs | 538 U.S. 721 | 2003 | Supreme Court of the United States | FMLA's family-care provisions validly abrogate state sovereign immunity under § 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment; situates the FMLA as anti-discrimination legislation. |

Canonical statutes: 29 U.S.C. § 2611; 29 U.S.C. § 2612; 29 U.S.C. § 2614; 29 C.F.R. § 825.110.
