## Bristol-Myers Squibb personal jurisdiction (specific-jurisdiction doctrine)

Specific personal jurisdiction requires a connection between the defendant's forum-state activity and each plaintiff's claim; circuits are split on whether BMS applies to absent class members in nationwide class actions.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bristol-Myers Squibb Co. v. Superior Court of California | 582 U.S. 255 | 2017 | Supreme Court of the United States | California courts lacked specific personal jurisdiction over BMS for claims by non-resident plaintiffs whose injuries had no connection to California, even though resident plaintiffs had similar claims. |
| Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court | 592 U.S. 351 | 2021 | Supreme Court of the United States | Specific jurisdiction exists when a defendant's in-state contacts 'relate to' the plaintiff's claim, even without strict causation; Ford's extensive sales/marketing of the same vehicle model in the forum sufficed. |
| Mallory v. Norfolk Southern Railway Co. | 600 U.S. 122 | 2023 | Supreme Court of the United States | A state law requiring out-of-state corporations to consent to general jurisdiction as a condition of doing business is constitutional under Pennsylvania Fire (1917), notwithstanding International Shoe's minimum-contacts framework. |

## Comparative anti-SLAPP statutes (Texas TCPA / California § 425.16)

Anti-SLAPP statutes vary substantially: California § 425.16 broadly protects 'protected activity' across the board; Texas's TCPA (Ch. 27 CPRC) was narrowed in 2019; circuits also split on whether state anti-SLAPP procedure applies in federal diversity.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Briggs v. Eden Council for Hope & Opportunity | 19 Cal. 4th 1106 | 1999 | Supreme Court of California | California's anti-SLAPP statute applies to all causes of action arising from acts in furtherance of constitutionally protected speech or petitioning, regardless of whether the plaintiff is a public figure or the speech is a matter of public interest. |
| Equilon Enterprises v. Consumer Cause, Inc. | 29 Cal. 4th 53 | 2002 | Supreme Court of California | Defendant invoking § 425.16 need only show the suit arises from protected activity; intent to chill speech is not a separate element. |

Canonical statutes: Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16; Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 27.001-27.011 (TCPA).

## Diversity jurisdiction (28 USC 1332)

When a federal court may hear a case based on the citizenship of parties under 28 U.S.C. § 1332.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strawbridge v. Curtiss | 7 U.S. (3 Cranch) 267 | 1806 | Supreme Court of the United States | Established the complete-diversity rule: every plaintiff must be a citizen of a different state from every defendant. |
| Hertz Corp. v. Friend | 559 U.S. 77 | 2010 | Supreme Court of the United States | A corporation's 'principal place of business' is its 'nerve center', typically corporate headquarters, not where its operations are concentrated. |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1332; 28 U.S.C. § 1367.

## Erie doctrine

The rule governing when federal courts sitting in diversity must apply state substantive law versus federal procedural law.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins | 304 U.S. 64 | 1938 | Supreme Court of the United States | Federal courts sitting in diversity must apply state substantive law; there is no general federal common law. |
| Hanna v. Plumer | 380 U.S. 460 | 1965 | Supreme Court of the United States | When a Federal Rule of Civil Procedure directly conflicts with state law and is constitutional under the Rules Enabling Act, the federal rule applies. |
| Guaranty Trust Co. v. York | 326 U.S. 99 | 1945 | Supreme Court of the United States | Adopted the outcome-determinative test: if applying federal procedure would significantly affect the outcome of the case, the federal court should follow state law. |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1652; 28 U.S.C. § 2072 (Rules Enabling Act).

## Federal appellate standards of review

The appellate standards governing legal, factual, and discretionary determinations on review.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anderson v. City of Bessemer City | 470 U.S. 564 | 1985 | Supreme Court of the United States | Findings of fact may be set aside only if clearly erroneous; the appellate court may not substitute its judgment when the trial court's account is plausible in light of the record viewed as a whole. |
| Ornelas v. United States | 517 U.S. 690 | 1996 | Supreme Court of the United States | Determinations of probable cause and reasonable suspicion are reviewed de novo; findings of historical fact for clear error. |
| Pierce v. Underwood | 487 U.S. 552 | 1988 | Supreme Court of the United States | Articulated the abuse-of-discretion standard, explaining that a district court's discretionary ruling will not be disturbed unless it rests on a clearly erroneous factual finding or an erroneous legal conclusion. |

## Improper removal attorney fees (28 U.S.C. § 1447(c))

An order remanding a removed case may award attorney fees only where the removing party lacked an objectively reasonable basis for seeking removal.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Martin v. Franklin Capital Corp. | 546 U.S. 132 | 2005 | Supreme Court of the United States | Absent unusual circumstances, attorney fees under § 1447(c) should be awarded only where the removing party lacked an objectively reasonable basis for seeking removal. |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).

## Shady Grove / Erie procedural-substantive analysis

When a Federal Rule directly conflicts with state law, the Federal Rule governs if valid under the Rules Enabling Act; absent a direct conflict, courts apply the Erie twin-aims and outcome-determinative tests.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shady Grove Orthopedic Associates, P.A. v. Allstate Insurance Co. | 559 U.S. 393 | 2010 | Supreme Court of the United States | When a Federal Rule of Civil Procedure answers the question in dispute, it governs unless invalid under the Rules Enabling Act. New York's prohibition on class actions for statutory penalties did not bar a federal Rule 23 class action in diversity. |
| Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc. | 518 U.S. 415 | 1996 | Supreme Court of the United States | In diversity, federal courts apply state-law standards for reviewing the excessiveness of jury verdicts, balanced against the Seventh Amendment's federal interest in jury-trial control via abuse-of-discretion review on appeal. |
| Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Cooperative, Inc. | 356 U.S. 525 | 1958 | Supreme Court of the United States | Even when state law might otherwise control, a strong federal interest (e.g., the federal jury-trial right) can override the state rule. |

## Summary judgment standard (FRCP 56)

The federal standard for granting or defeating a motion for summary judgment under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Celotex Corp. v. Catrett | 477 U.S. 317 | 1986 | Supreme Court of the United States | The moving party may meet its burden by showing that the nonmovant lacks evidence essential to its case; no requirement to negate the nonmovant's claims affirmatively. |
| Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc. | 477 U.S. 242 | 1986 | Supreme Court of the United States | A genuine dispute exists if a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmovant; courts apply the substantive evidentiary standard at this stage. |
| Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp. | 475 U.S. 574 | 1986 | Supreme Court of the United States | The nonmovant must come forward with specific facts; the inferences drawn must be reasonable in light of the record as a whole. |

Canonical statutes: Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a).

## Transfer of venue (28 USC 1404(a))

Transfer of a federal civil action to another district under 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) and the Atlantic Marine framework.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. United States District Court | 571 U.S. 49 | 2013 | Supreme Court of the United States | When a forum-selection clause is enforceable, transfer under § 1404(a) ordinarily follows: plaintiff's choice of forum gets no weight, only public-interest factors are considered, and the original venue's choice-of-law rules do not follow the case. |
| Van Dusen v. Barrack | 376 U.S. 612 | 1964 | Supreme Court of the United States | On a § 1404(a) transfer initiated by the defendant, the transferee court must apply the choice-of-law rules of the transferor court so that the change of forum is one of courtrooms, not law. |
| Stewart Organization, Inc. v. Ricoh Corp. | 487 U.S. 22 | 1988 | Supreme Court of the United States | Federal law (§ 1404(a)) governs the enforceability of forum-selection clauses in federal court; the clause is one factor among many in the convenience analysis (later effectively superseded by Atlantic Marine). |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a); 28 U.S.C. § 1406(a).

## Twombly/Iqbal plausibility pleading standard

The federal pleading standard requiring a complaint to state a claim that is plausible on its face.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly | 550 U.S. 544 | 2007 | Supreme Court of the United States | A complaint must contain enough factual matter to state a claim that is plausible on its face; conclusory allegations are insufficient. |
| Ashcroft v. Iqbal | 556 U.S. 662 | 2009 | Supreme Court of the United States | Twombly applies to all civil cases; courts disregard conclusory legal allegations and assess only well-pleaded factual content for plausibility. |
| Conley v. Gibson | 355 U.S. 41 | 1957 | Supreme Court of the United States | Historical context: previously articulated the 'no set of facts' notice-pleading standard that Twombly retired; still cited to mark the doctrinal shift. |

Canonical statutes: Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(a)(2); Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6).

## Well-pleaded complaint rule

The rule that federal-question jurisdiction must appear on the face of a properly pleaded complaint.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co. v. Mottley | 211 U.S. 149 | 1908 | Supreme Court of the United States | Federal-question jurisdiction exists only when the federal issue appears on the face of the plaintiff's well-pleaded complaint; an anticipated federal defense does not suffice. |
| Grable & Sons Metal Products, Inc. v. Darue Engineering & Manufacturing | 545 U.S. 308 | 2005 | Supreme Court of the United States | A state-law claim may invoke federal-question jurisdiction if it necessarily raises a substantial, actually disputed federal issue that may be entertained without disturbing the federal-state balance. |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1331.

## ZF Automotive v. Luxshare - § 1782 reach to private commercial arbitration

Discovery under 28 U.S.C. § 1782 is unavailable for private commercial arbitration tribunals; the statute reaches only governmental or intergovernmental adjudicative bodies.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ZF Automotive US, Inc. v. Luxshare, Ltd. | 596 U.S. 619 | 2022 | Supreme Court of the United States | A 'foreign or international tribunal' under 28 U.S.C. § 1782 means a governmental or intergovernmental adjudicative body; § 1782 discovery is unavailable for purely private commercial arbitration. |

Canonical statutes: 28 U.S.C. § 1782.
