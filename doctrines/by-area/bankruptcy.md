## Chapter 11 plan releases - 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(6) + Harrington v. Purdue

Chapter 11 plans may include 'any other appropriate provision not inconsistent with the applicable provisions of this title' under § 1123(b)(6), but Harrington v. Purdue (2024) confirms this catchall cannot authorize non-consensual third-party (non-debtor) releases.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P. | 603 U.S. 204 | 2024 | Supreme Court of the United States | The Bankruptcy Code does not authorize non-consensual releases of third-party claims against non-debtors as part of a Chapter 11 plan; § 1123(b)(6)'s catchall does not stretch that far. |
| In re Metromedia Fiber Network, Inc. | 416 F.3d 136 | 2005 | Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit | Pre-Harrington Second Circuit framework for non-debtor releases: permitted only in 'rare cases' supported by specific factors. Harrington largely supersedes this for non-consensual releases. |
| In re Continental Airlines | 203 F.3d 203 | 2000 | Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit | Pre-Harrington Third Circuit position: rejected non-consensual third-party releases absent extraordinary circumstances. Harrington adopts a near-categorical bar in line with this view. |

Canonical statutes: 11 U.S.C. § 1123(b)(6); 11 U.S.C. § 524(e).

## Harrington v. Purdue Pharma - non-consensual third-party releases in Chapter 11

Chapter 11 reorganization plans may not provide non-consensual releases of claims against non-debtor third parties; the Code does not authorize such releases.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P. | 603 U.S. 204 | 2024 | Supreme Court of the United States | The Bankruptcy Code does not authorize non-consensual releases of third-party claims against non-debtors (such as the Sackler family) as part of a Chapter 11 reorganization plan. |

## MOAC Mall Holdings v. Transform - Bankruptcy Code § 363(m) is non-jurisdictional

Section 363(m) of the Bankruptcy Code is a statutory limitation on relief, not a jurisdictional bar; an appellate court does not lose jurisdiction merely because a sale has closed.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MOAC Mall Holdings LLC v. Transform Holdco LLC | 598 U.S. 288 | 2023 | Supreme Court of the United States | Bankruptcy Code § 363(m) is not jurisdictional; the failure to obtain a stay pending appeal does not deprive a federal appellate court of subject-matter jurisdiction over the appeal. |
