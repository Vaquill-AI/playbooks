## Private Rule 10b-5 securities-fraud claim

The six elements of a private securities-fraud action under Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Halliburton Co. v. Erica P. John Fund, Inc. | 573 U.S. 258 | 2014 | Supreme Court of the United States | Six elements: material misrepresentation or omission; scienter; connection with the purchase or sale of a security; reliance; economic loss; and loss causation. |
| Basic Inc. v. Levinson | 485 U.S. 224 | 1988 | Supreme Court of the United States | Reliance may be presumed in a fraud-on-the-market case for securities traded on an efficient market. |
| Dura Pharmaceuticals, Inc. v. Broudo | 544 U.S. 336 | 2005 | Supreme Court of the United States | Loss causation requires that the defendant's misrepresentation actually caused the plaintiff's economic loss; an inflated purchase price alone is insufficient. |

Canonical statutes: 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b); 17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5.

## PSLRA pleading standard for scienter

The heightened pleading standard for scienter in private securities-fraud actions under 15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(2).

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tellabs, Inc. v. Makor Issues & Rights, Ltd. | 551 U.S. 308 | 2007 | Supreme Court of the United States | To plead scienter under the PSLRA, the complaint must state with particularity facts giving rise to a strong inference, at least as compelling as any opposing inference of nonfraudulent intent. |
| Matrixx Initiatives, Inc. v. Siracusano | 563 U.S. 27 | 2011 | Supreme Court of the United States | Applying Tellabs, the Court held that allegations of undisclosed adverse-event reports may give rise to a strong inference of scienter when, taken collectively, they are at least as compelling as an innocent inference. |

Canonical statutes: 15 U.S.C. § 78u-4(b)(2).

## Slack Technologies v. Pirani - Section 11 traceability in direct listings

A Section 11 plaintiff must plead and prove that the specific shares purchased were 'such security' registered pursuant to the challenged registration statement.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slack Technologies, LLC v. Pirani | 598 U.S. 759 | 2023 | Supreme Court of the United States | Section 11 of the Securities Act of 1933 requires a plaintiff to plead and prove that they purchased shares registered under the misleading registration statement - a meaningful constraint for direct listings where registered and unregistered shares trade together. |
