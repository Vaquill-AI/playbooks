## Copyright fair use (17 USC 107)

The four-factor copyright fair-use test under 17 U.S.C. § 107.

Landmark cases:

| Case | Citation | Year | Court | Holding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc. | 510 U.S. 569 | 1994 | Supreme Court of the United States | Parody can be transformative and qualify as fair use; the four factors are weighed together, with no single factor dispositive; the more transformative the use, the less significant the other factors. |
| Harper & Row Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises | 471 U.S. 539 | 1985 | Supreme Court of the United States | Fair-use analysis weighs the purpose, nature, amount, and market effect of the use; commercial use is presumptively unfair; effect on the market is the single most important element. |
| Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc. | 593 U.S. 1 | 2021 | Supreme Court of the United States | Google's copying of the Java SE API to build a new platform was transformative and qualified as fair use; reaffirmed Campbell's flexible balancing of the four factors in the software context. |

Canonical statutes: 17 U.S.C. § 107.
