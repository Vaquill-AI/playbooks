## Severability (mutual, with good-faith reformation to nearest enforceable intent)

**Tone:** balanced

### Clause text

Severability. If any term, covenant, or provision of this Agreement, or its application to any person or circumstance, is held by a court or other tribunal of competent jurisdiction to be invalid, illegal, void, or unenforceable in any respect, then (a) that holding will not affect, impair, or invalidate any other term, covenant, or provision of this Agreement or the application of the affected provision to any other person or circumstance, and (b) the remainder of this Agreement will continue in full force and effect and will be enforced to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, as if the invalid, illegal, void, or unenforceable provision had never been contained in it. Upon a determination that any provision is invalid, illegal, void, or unenforceable, the parties will negotiate in good faith to modify this Agreement so as to effect their original intent as closely as possible to the greatest extent permitted by law, in order that the transactions contemplated by this Agreement be consummated as originally contemplated to the fullest extent possible. To that end, each party agrees that the court or tribunal making the determination is authorized and requested to reform, blue-pencil, or otherwise modify the affected provision to the minimum extent necessary to render it valid, legal, and enforceable while preserving as nearly as possible the economic and legal substance and intent of the original provision; if a provision is held unenforceable because it is excessive in scope, duration, geographic reach, or degree, the provision will be deemed amended to the maximum scope, duration, reach, or degree that is enforceable, and the provision will be enforced as so amended. Notwithstanding the foregoing, if severance or reformation of a provision would materially frustrate the essential purpose of this Agreement or deprive either party of the substantial benefit of its bargain, the parties will negotiate in good faith an equitable adjustment to the affected terms.
