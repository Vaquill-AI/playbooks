## Confidentiality (mutual, 3-year term, perpetual for trade secrets)

**Tone:** balanced

### Clause text

(a) Definition. "Confidential Information" means non-public information disclosed by or on behalf of one party (the "Disclosing Party") to the other (the "Receiving Party") in connection with the Purpose, whether disclosed in writing, orally, visually, electronically, or by inspection, and whether or not marked or identified as confidential, including business, financial, technical, and product information, pricing, customer and supplier data, source code, know-how, and trade secrets. Confidential Information does not include a person's general knowledge, skill, training, or experience.

(b) Exclusions. Confidential Information does not include information that: (i) is or becomes publicly available through no act or omission of the Receiving Party; (ii) was lawfully known to the Receiving Party before disclosure, as shown by its records; (iii) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Confidential Information; or (iv) is rightfully received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality.

(c) Obligations. The Receiving Party shall use the Confidential Information solely for the Purpose, shall protect it using no less than reasonable care, and shall limit access to its employees, affiliates, and advisors ("Representatives") who need to know it for the Purpose and are bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than these. The Receiving Party is responsible for any breach by its Representatives.

(d) Compelled Disclosure. If the Receiving Party is required by law or valid legal process to disclose Confidential Information, it shall, to the extent legally permitted, give the Disclosing Party prompt written notice and reasonable cooperation to seek a protective order, and shall disclose only the portion legally required.

(e) Term. Confidentiality obligations continue for three (3) years after disclosure of the applicable item, except that obligations as to information that constitutes a trade secret continue for so long as it remains a trade secret under applicable law.

(f) Return or Destruction. Upon the Disclosing Party's written request or termination, the Receiving Party shall return or destroy Confidential Information in its possession, except for copies retained in routine backups or as required by law, which remain subject to this clause. Nothing in this clause limits any rights or remedies under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. §§ 1836-1839, or applicable state trade-secret law.

### Applicable acts and authorities

- DTSA (18 U.S.C. 1836)
- 18 U.S.C. 1839
- Uniform Trade Secrets Act (state)

## Confidentiality (protective, discloser-favorable, perpetual trade secrets, DTSA notice)

**Tone:** protective

### Clause text

(a) Definition. "Confidential Information" means all non-public information disclosed by or on behalf of the Disclosing Party to the Receiving Party, before or after the Effective Date, in any form and whether or not marked confidential, including the existence and terms of this Agreement, business plans, financial and pricing information, customer and prospect lists, source code, algorithms, designs, processes, know-how, and trade secrets, together with all notes, analyses, and derivatives the Receiving Party prepares that contain or are based on such information.

(b) Exclusions. The Receiving Party bears the burden of proving, by contemporaneous written records, that information falls within an exclusion. Confidential Information does not include information that: (i) is or becomes public through no act or omission of the Receiving Party or its Representatives; (ii) was rightfully in the Receiving Party's possession, free of any duty of confidentiality, before disclosure; (iii) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without any use of or reference to the Confidential Information; or (iv) is rightfully obtained from a third party with no duty of confidentiality to the Disclosing Party.

(c) Obligations. The Receiving Party shall (i) hold the Confidential Information in strict confidence and use at least the degree of care it uses for its own most sensitive information, but never less than reasonable care; (ii) use it solely to perform under this Agreement and for no other purpose; (iii) not disclose it except to Representatives who have a strict need to know and are bound in writing to obligations at least as protective as these; (iv) not reverse engineer, decompile, or disassemble any disclosed item; and (v) promptly notify the Disclosing Party of any actual or suspected unauthorized use or disclosure. The Receiving Party is fully liable for its Representatives' acts and omissions. No residual-knowledge or implied license is granted, and all Confidential Information is provided "as is."

(d) Compelled Disclosure. If compelled by law or legal process to disclose, the Receiving Party shall, unless legally prohibited, give the Disclosing Party prompt written notice and reasonable cooperation, at the Disclosing Party's expense, to seek confidential treatment or a protective order, and shall disclose only the minimum portion legally required.

(e) DTSA Notice. Under 18 U.S.C. 1833(b), an individual will not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade-secret law for disclosing a trade secret that is made in confidence to a government official or attorney solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or in a sealed court filing. Nothing in this Agreement limits any individual's right to report possible violations to, or communicate with, any governmental agency, including under 17 C.F.R. 240.21F-17.

(f) Term; Return. Obligations survive for five (5) years after termination, and indefinitely as to any item that qualifies as a trade secret for as long as it remains a trade secret. Upon request or termination, the Receiving Party shall promptly return or destroy all Confidential Information and, if requested, certify the destruction in writing signed by an officer, subject only to archival backups retained under legal-hold or records-retention obligations, which remain protected.

### Applicable acts and authorities

- DTSA (18 U.S.C. 1836)
- 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)
- 18 U.S.C. 1839
- 17 C.F.R. 240.21F-17
- Uniform Trade Secrets Act (state)

## Confidentiality (permissive, marking required, 2-year term, light-touch)

**Tone:** permissive

### Clause text

(a) Definition. "Confidential Information" means information that the Disclosing Party discloses to the Receiving Party and that is either marked or identified as "Confidential" at the time of disclosure or, if disclosed orally or visually, is identified as confidential when disclosed and summarized in writing to the Receiving Party within thirty (30) days. Confidential Information does not include the Receiving Party's general knowledge, skill, training, or experience, including ideas, concepts, and techniques retained in the unaided memory of its personnel.

(b) Exclusions. Confidential Information does not include information that: (i) is or becomes publicly available other than through the Receiving Party's breach; (ii) was known to the Receiving Party before disclosure; (iii) is independently developed by the Receiving Party; or (iv) is received from a third party without a known duty of confidentiality.

(c) Obligations. The Receiving Party shall use reasonable care, no less than it uses to protect its own similar information, to avoid unauthorized disclosure of Confidential Information, and shall use it for the Purpose. The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to its employees, affiliates, contractors, and professional advisors who need it and are subject to confidentiality duties, and may disclose it as reasonably necessary to perform under this Agreement. The Receiving Party may also disclose Confidential Information where required by law or legal process, and will, where practicable and lawful, notify the Disclosing Party so it may seek protective treatment.

(d) Term. The Receiving Party's obligations apply for two (2) years from the date each item is disclosed, after which the Receiving Party has no further obligation with respect to that item, except that information constituting a trade secret remains protected for as long as it qualifies as a trade secret under applicable law, including the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. 1836 et seq.

(e) No Other Restrictions; Return. Nothing in this clause restricts either party from independently developing or marketing products or services, or from entering similar arrangements with others, provided it does not breach this clause. Upon the Disclosing Party's written request after termination, the Receiving Party will return or destroy the Confidential Information then in its possession, except for copies in routine system backups or retained as required by law or internal compliance policy, which remain subject to confidentiality until deleted in the ordinary course.

### Applicable acts and authorities

- DTSA (18 U.S.C. 1836)
- Uniform Trade Secrets Act (state)
